1. Define the Scope of GenAI Resiliency

Before jumping into solutions, clarify:

Operational resiliency: uptime, failover, fallback when models/services fail.

Data resiliency: protecting training data, embeddings, and prompts against corruption, leakage, or loss.

Model resiliency: ensuring model availability (primary/backup), monitoring drift, detecting adversarial use.

Business resiliency: continuity of AI-enabled processes if a provider or service becomes unavailable.

Compliance resiliency: how the org adapts to changing regulations (AI Act, NIST, etc.).

3. Implementation Steps

Here’s a practical roadmap you can present:

A. Assessment

Inventory where GenAI is currently being used across teams.

Identify single points of failure (e.g., single vendor lock-in, no backup models).

Map out data flow: ingestion → processing → model → output → consumer.

B. Design for Resiliency

Multi-model strategy:

Primary LLM (e.g., OpenAI/Anthropic)

Backup open-source model (Llama, Mistral) fine-tuned for core tasks.

Fallback systems:

If GenAI fails, users still get keyword search, rules-based answers, or cached outputs.

Redundancy in infrastructure:

Multi-cloud or hybrid deployment.

Vector DB replication across regions.

C. Monitoring & Controls

Continuous monitoring for hallucinations, bias, and drift.

Incident response playbook for GenAI outages.

Logging + observability tailored for LLMs (latency, token usage, error rates).

D. Compliance & Security

Ensure sensitive data masking before prompts.

Role-based access to models and embeddings.

Regular audits for regulatory compliance.

E. People & Processes

Training employees on fallback workflows when GenAI tools are unavailable.

Establishing an AI Resiliency Council (cross-team governance).

Running chaos-engineering-style “AI outage drills.”

4. Deliverables You Can Prepare
